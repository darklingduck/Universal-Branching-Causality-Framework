# Temporal Probability and "Fate"

## Purpose

Preserve the distinction between ordinary branching and the special use of probability when a traveler skips causal events.

## Ordinary Causality

Probability is not used to decide normal branches in UBCF. An observer progresses through events and encounters each Event Branch Node in sequence.

## Skipping Causality

Probability becomes relevant when an observer bypasses intermediate Event Branch Nodes, such as by traveling directly into the future.

The traveler does not experience or participate in all of the intervening causal events. As a result, the destination branch is not selected by the traveler's knowledge or preference.

## Cosmic Randomness

If a time-travel device merely changes temporal position without mapping and controlling a complete causal path, the traveler arrives in a future branch by what was informally described as "Fate": a cosmic roll of the dice.

The word "Fate" does not imply a conscious force. It is shorthand for the unresolved selection of one future branch among the enormous branching structure at the destination time.

## No Compatibility Requirement

A destination branch does not need to be comfortable, safe, familiar, or compatible with the traveler.

The traveler may emerge into a branch containing war, collapse, radically different social conditions, or other outcomes that make the destination unexpected or dangerous.

## Causality Mapping

A hypothetical system capable of mapping branches by their causal choices might allow targeted navigation toward a desired branch-like history. It still cannot return the traveler to the originating branch, because departure and arrival create new causal divergences.

Even attempting to return to the exact moment of original departure creates another branch at that point rather than restoring the original Causal Lineage.
