# UBCF Examples Archive

These examples preserve scenarios used during development. They are explanatory tools, not evidence for the framework.

## Brain Cell / ICS Divergence
A single abnormal cell has several possible physical continuations. It may attach in a location that affects the brain, be swept out of the body, or travel to another distinguishable location. Each outcome changes the future causal state and may therefore affect the ICS differently.

The important point is that immediately before the causal divergence, the histories and ICS states are identical. The difference is added at the Event Branch Node.

## One Million Direct Outcomes
A person is presented with one million possible partners and must directly choose one. If all one million selections are genuine distinguishable outcomes of one causal event, the EBN may have one million continuations.

## Hierarchical Elimination
A person is presented with one million possible partners but must first eliminate 50%, then make additional reductions through later decisions. This is not the same structure as the previous example. Each elimination stage is a separate EBN with its own outcomes.

## Meteor Deflection
A meteor is traveling toward Earth. A sequence of unrelated physical events causes another object to strike it and alter its trajectory. No creature made a conscious choice, but the physical causal outcomes are distinguishable. UBCF therefore does not restrict branching to conscious agency.

## Rolling Ball and Rocks
A ball rolling across uneven ground may encounter rocks or other physical differences that alter its path. Reality contains the underlying interactions, while the UBCF model represents distinguishable causal deviations as branch outcomes rather than requiring every descriptive level to be treated as a separate human-observed event.

## Remote Branching
An event in a distant region of the universe may branch even if Earth never receives information about it. Branching is universal rather than observer-centered.

## Time Traveler Departure
A traveler leaves at a temporal point. The departure is itself a causal change. A branch continues from that point in which the traveler is absent.

## Time Traveler Arrival
The traveler appears at another temporal point. Their arrival introduces a causal event that was not part of the pre-arrival lineage and therefore creates another branch.

## Two Travelers / Two Ages
After backward time travel, a younger branch-local version and an older arriving version of the traveler may coexist. They share historical identity up to the relevant divergence but are separate causal individuals from that point forward.

## Attempted Return Home
Even if a traveler could map the sequence of choices in the originating Causal Lineage, following those choices and arriving at the exact apparent departure moment would add the arrival itself to history. The result is therefore a new branch, not the original.

## Forward Jump
A traveler skips intermediate Event Branch Nodes and chooses only a destination time. The future reached is not required to be comfortable or compatible. It may contain war, extinction, prosperity, technological change, or other outcomes. The traveler has not personally traversed the intermediate causal choices.
