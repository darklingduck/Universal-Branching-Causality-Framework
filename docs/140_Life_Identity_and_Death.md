# Life, Identity, and Death in UBCF

## Purpose

Preserve the working model for conception, birth, identity, life, and death within a branching causal structure.

## Beginning of an ICS

UBCF does not presently know or define the exact point at which an Identity Continuity Structure first comes into being.

Conception, birth, development of awareness, or another causal threshold may be relevant, but none is currently designated as the universal beginning of an ICS.

Because Causal Lineages differ, the progenitors and causal conditions associated with related identities may meet or develop at different times and with different prior experiences. Identity formation is therefore dependent on branch-specific causality.

## Conception

Conception can form a branch-specific biological instance whose later state depends on its Causal Lineage. It is an important biological event but is not currently defined by UBCF as the universal beginning of ICS.

Because different branches can contain different causal histories, conception and later development may occur differently across branches.

## Birth

Birth marks a major transition into independent experiential interaction with the environment. It is not treated as the absolute beginning of identity, but as an important causal threshold in the development of a branch-local organism and its experiences.

## Life

Life is the ongoing continuation of a branch-local ICS through successive causal events. Biological state, memory, environment, interactions, and choices all contribute to that continuation.

## Death

Death is an endpoint for the local continuation of an ICS in a specific branch.

UBCF does not claim knowledge of an afterlife or what, if anything, occurs after death. This is an explicit epistemic boundary of the framework.

## ICS Lineage Set Continuation

An individual ICS can end in one branch while related ICS continuations persist in other branches. The collection of branch-local continuations sharing a common ancestral identity history is called an **ICS Lineage Set**.

An ICS Lineage Set could eventually have no continuing members, or could theoretically continue indefinitely if some branch contains causal conditions that preserve life or awareness.

An example discussed was a branch in which a life-extending chemical is developed, producing a longer continuation of an ICS within that lineage set.

## Identity Across Branches

Multiple branch-local identities may be valid continuations of the same shared pre-branch identity state. After divergence, each develops independently through its own Causal Lineage.
