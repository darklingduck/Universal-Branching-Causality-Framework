# Time Travel in UBCF

## General Principle

Time travel does not move an individual back into their original causal history. The act of time travel creates new branches.

## Departure Branch

When a traveler leaves their originating branch, the original branch continues with the traveler absent.

## Arrival Branch

When the traveler arrives at another point in time, a new branch is created at the point of arrival.

## Returning to the Original Branch

A causality map would not allow a traveler to truly return to the original branch. The traveler could only create another branch with similar conditions.

## Forward Time Travel

Forward travel skips intermediate causal events. Because those events were not experienced by the traveler, the arrival point is determined by the causal possibilities of the destination time rather than by the traveler's intent.

## Sideways Travel

Travel between branches follows the same principle. The act of entering another branch creates a new causal event.
