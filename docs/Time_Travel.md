# Time Travel in UBCF

## General Principle

Time travel does not move an individual back into their original causal history. The act of temporal displacement creates new branches.

## Departure Branch

When a traveler leaves their originating branch, the originating branch continues with the traveler absent.

The departure event therefore produces at least two distinguishable continuations: one in which the traveler does not depart and one in which the traveler does.

## Arrival Branch

When the traveler arrives at another point in time, the arrival itself is a causal alteration and creates a new branch.

The unaltered continuation in which the traveler does not arrive remains.

## Returning to the Original Branch

A causality map would not allow a traveler to truly return to the originating branch. The traveler could only create another branch with similar conditions.

Even arrival at the exact apparent instant of original departure adds a new causal event and therefore creates another branch.

## Forward Time Travel

True forward temporal displacement skips intermediate causal participation. The traveler is absent from the intervening Event Branch Nodes and later re-enters the causal structure.

This invokes Skipped-Causality Branch Resolution (SCBR). The traveler may choose the temporal destination, but does not thereby choose the destination branch.

## Relativistic Travel

Relativistic time dilation is not treated as a true causal skip. A relativistic traveler may experience less proper time than another observer while retaining a continuous Causal Lineage through the interval.

Because causal participation remains continuous, relativistic travel does not invoke SCBR.

## Backward Time Travel

A traveler may move to an earlier temporal position, but time within each branch remains forward-ordered. The traveler does not make the branch's time run backward.

Arrival at the earlier point creates a new causal continuation.

## Sideways Travel

Travel between branches follows the same causal-alteration rule. Departure and entry are causal events, and entry cannot overwrite an existing continuation.
