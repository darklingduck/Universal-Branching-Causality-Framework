# Life, Identity, and Death in UBCF

## Purpose

Preserve the working model for conception, birth, identity, life, and death within a branching causal structure.

## Conception

Conception was described as the formation of a branch-specific biological instance whose later state depends on its Causal Lineage.

Because different branches can contain different causal histories, conception and later development may occur differently across branches.

## Birth

Birth marks a major transition into independent experiential interaction with the environment. It is not treated as the absolute beginning of identity, but as an important causal threshold in the development of an Identity Continuity Structure.

## Life

Life is the ongoing traversal of a branch through successive causal events. Biological state, memory, environment, interactions, and choices all contribute to the continuing ICS.

## Death

Death is an endpoint for the local continuation of an ICS in a specific branch.

UBCF does not claim knowledge of an afterlife or what, if anything, occurs after death. This is an explicit epistemic boundary of the framework.

## Total ICS Continuation

An ICS can end in one branch while continuing in others. The total set of branch-specific continuations can also eventually end, or could theoretically continue indefinitely if some branch contains causal conditions that preserve life.

An example discussed was a branch in which a life-extending chemical is developed, producing a longer continuation of that ICS.

## Identity Across Branches

Multiple versions of a person are valid continuations of the same pre-branch identity. After divergence, each develops independently through its own Causal Lineage.
