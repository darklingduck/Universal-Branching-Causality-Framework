# Moral Branching and Neutrality

## Purpose

Preserve the early moral model that helped lead to UBCF while keeping it distinct from the later causal framework.

## Neutral Origin

The early model proposed that an identity or soul is not inherently good or evil at origin. It begins neutral, with later moral direction emerging through choices, environment, and causal history.

## Divergent Moral Outcomes

Different continuations of the same pre-branch identity may develop toward different moral states.

One continuation may become more altruistic, another more harmful, and another remain comparatively neutral.

## Good, Evil, and Neutrality

A recurring metaphor described good and evil as opposite faces of a coin, with neutrality as the edge that may tip toward either direction.

The point of the metaphor was not that morality is numerically balanced, but that moral character develops through causal history rather than being fixed at origin.

## Relationship to UBCF

This moral model is historical context rather than a required physical axiom of UBCF.

Its importance is conceptual: it introduced the idea that multiple valid continuations of the same origin can develop very differently depending on their Causal Lineages.

## Free Will and Constraint

The discussion acknowledged that choices are influenced by biology, upbringing, environment, information, and previous events. UBCF therefore does not require an unconstrained form of free will.

Agency can operate within a causal history without implying that choices occur independently of all prior causes.

## Religious Interpretation

A theological interpretation may identify the Identity Continuity Structure with a soul, while a materialist interpretation may treat it as informational and experiential continuity. UBCF itself does not require either interpretation.
