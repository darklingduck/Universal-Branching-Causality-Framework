# Skipped-Causality Branch Resolution (SCBR)

## Definition

Skipped-Causality Branch Resolution is the UBCF mechanism governing the causal destination of an entity that ceases causal participation across an interval and later re-enters the causal structure without a continuous Causal Lineage through the intervening Event Branch Nodes.

SCBR does not describe normal branching. In ordinary causality, every distinguishable outcome continues as its own branch and no branch is selected as uniquely real.

## Causal Participation

A causal skip is not defined by lack of knowledge, observation, consciousness, or memory.

An entity may sleep, be unconscious, remain isolated, or be unaware of distant events while still participating continuously in causality.

SCBR applies only when the entity lacks continuous causal participation through the intervening branching structure.

## Primary Application: Forward Time Travel Branch Resolution

A hypothetical true forward-time displacement that removes a traveler from causal participation at one temporal point and reintroduces the traveler at a later temporal point bypasses the intermediate Event Branch Nodes.

Because the traveler has no continuous branch-local Causal Lineage through that interval, the arrival continuation must be resolved among the causal possibilities present at the destination time.

The traveler chooses the temporal destination, not the destination branch, unless a separate mechanism exists for mapping and targeting causal histories.

## Probability

Probability is not used to decide which ordinary branch is real.

Within SCBR, probability represents the relative likelihood of destination continuations based on the causal conditions that developed during the skipped interval. The precise mathematical resolution rule remains an open question.

The informal term "Fate" has previously been used for this cosmic-scale unresolved outcome. It does not imply a conscious force.

## Relativistic Travel Is Not SCBR

Relativistic travel may cause a traveler to experience substantially less proper time than observers in another reference frame. This can resemble forward time travel experientially, but the traveler retains a continuous worldline and remains causally involved in the branching structure.

Therefore, reduced experienced time is not sufficient to invoke SCBR.

The distinction is:

- **Reduced or different experienced time with continuous causal participation:** no SCBR.
- **Discontinuous causal participation followed by later re-entry:** SCBR.

## Causality Mapping

A hypothetical system capable of mapping causal histories might permit targeted travel toward a desired type of destination continuation. Such targeting would be a separate navigation mechanism, not SCBR itself.

Even perfect causal mapping cannot restore an originating branch. Departure and arrival are causal events that create additional divergence.
