# Time and Causality

## Time Within UBCF

UBCF describes time as the ordering structure of causal events.

Within a branch, this causal ordering remains forward. Time itself does not loop backward. A traveler may move to an earlier temporal position, but doing so creates new causal continuations rather than reversing the branch's own time order.

## Limits of Observation

An observer inside time cannot determine what exists outside time because leaving time would either require entering another temporal structure or experiencing a condition that cannot be observed from within the system.

## Before Time

Questions about what existed before time remain open, similar to questions about conditions before the Big Bang.

## Causality

Branches develop from causal relationships. Time provides the ordering through which Causal Lineage is formed.

## Causal Participation

An entity remains part of a branch's causal development so long as its Causal Lineage continues through the intervening events, even if the entity does not know about or directly observe those events.

Sleep, unconsciousness, isolation, limited perception, and differing experienced time do not by themselves constitute skipped causality.

## Relativistic Time Dilation

Relativistic travel can cause different observers to accumulate different amounts of proper time while remaining causally continuous.

A relativistic traveler therefore remains part of the causal structure and its branching history. Experiencing less elapsed time than another observer is not equivalent to leaving causality and does not invoke Skipped-Causality Branch Resolution (SCBR).

## True Causal Skips

A true causal skip requires discontinuous causal participation: an entity leaves causal participation across an interval and later re-enters without a continuous branch-local Causal Lineage through the intervening Event Branch Nodes.

Such cases invoke SCBR.
