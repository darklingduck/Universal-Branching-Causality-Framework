# Framework Design Decisions

This file records why major UBCF concepts currently work the way they do. These decisions may later be refined, but their history should remain preserved.

## FDD-001 — Branching Is Not Limited to Conscious Choice
**Status:** Accepted

An Event Branch Node may result from any causal event with multiple distinguishable outcomes. A conscious agent is not required. Meteor interactions, biological events, stellar processes, and other physical events can branch.

## FDD-002 — Thought Alone Does Not Create an External Branch
**Status:** Accepted

A possibility considered internally does not by itself create a new physical branch. The choice must enter the physical Causal Lineage through a causal change.

## FDD-003 — Event Branch Nodes Are Not Necessarily Binary
**Status:** Accepted

An EBN may have two, several, millions, or another number of distinguishable outcomes. Sequential elimination or filtering consists of multiple EBNs rather than one artificial million-way node.

## FDD-004 — Branches Share Their Past
**Status:** Accepted

Branching begins at the EBN. The history before the divergence remains one shared Causal Lineage rather than being duplicated backward to creation.

## FDD-005 — Branches Do Not Merge
**Status:** Accepted

After divergence, each branch has a unique Causal Lineage. Even if two later states appear identical, their histories remain different.

## FDD-006 — ICS Copies Are Equal Immediately Before Divergence
**Status:** Accepted

At the last instant before a branch-defining causal change, the resulting ICS continuations are exact copies. Each then acquires the information associated with its branch.

## FDD-007 — Probability Does Not Select Normal Branches
**Status:** Accepted

Ordinary branching does not use probability to choose which outcome becomes real. Distinguishable causal continuations branch.

Probability becomes relevant in special cases such as forward temporal displacement where the traveler skips intermediate causal events.

## FDD-008 — Time Travel Branches at Departure and Arrival
**Status:** Accepted

Leaving an originating branch produces a causal continuation in which the traveler is absent. Arrival at another temporal point introduces another causal change and therefore another branch.

## FDD-009 — The Original Branch Cannot Be Re-entered
**Status:** Accepted

Even perfect causal mapping cannot restore a traveler to the exact originating branch. Any arrival introduces an additional causal event. A traveler might reproduce a similar lineage and arrive at the apparent departure point, but that act creates another branch.

## FDD-010 — Forward Temporal Destination Is Not Required to Be Compatible
**Status:** Accepted

A traveler skipping forward may arrive in a radically different future, including a hostile, devastated, or otherwise unexpected branch. Compatibility with the traveler is not a selection requirement.

## FDD-011 — Global Impossibility Is Not Yet Defined
**Status:** Open / constrained

What appears impossible under the physical laws of one branch may not necessarily be impossible across every conceivable branch or nested universe. UBCF therefore avoids declaring current local physical laws universally mandatory without further justification.
