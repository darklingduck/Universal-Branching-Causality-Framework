# Identity Continuity Structure (ICS)

## Definition

The Identity Continuity Structure describes one branch-local continuation of awareness and identity through a causal structure.

UBCF does not presently claim to know the exact event at which an ICS first comes into being. Conception, birth, the development of awareness, or another causal threshold may be relevant, but the framework does not designate any one of them as the universal beginning of an ICS.

Because causal histories differ between branches, the progenitors and conditions that produce related identities may meet or develop at different times and with different prior experiences. Identity formation is therefore branch-dependent.

## Branching Event

Immediately before an Event Branch Node, there is one shared ICS state within the shared Causal Lineage.

At divergence, each resulting branch inherits an exact copy of that pre-branch ICS state. From that point onward, each copy accumulates different causal information and experiences only its own branch-local continuation.

No pre-existing observer chooses which branch to follow.

## ICS Lineage Set

An **ICS Lineage Set** is the collection of branch-local ICS continuations that share a common ancestral identity history before divergence.

The term distinguishes the aggregate family of related continuations from an individual ICS. An ICS is always branch-local; the ICS Lineage Set describes the related continuations collectively.

An individual ICS may terminate while other members of its ICS Lineage Set continue. The framework does not currently establish whether every ICS Lineage Set must eventually terminate.

## Example

A single cell in the brain may have multiple possible futures:

- In one branch it attaches to a critical location.
- In another branch it is removed by normal biological processes.
- In other branches it follows different paths.

Each distinguishable outcome creates a different causal continuation of the ICS.

## Continuity

An ICS does not experience all branches equally after divergence. Each post-divergence ICS experiences only the causal continuation of its own branch.

An ICS experiences and interprets local physical and temporal conditions from within its causal structure. Its interpretation does not create the underlying laws or causal ordering.

## Termination

An ICS within a branch may end. The continuation of an ICS depends on the causal history of that branch.
