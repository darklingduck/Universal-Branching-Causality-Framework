# Nested Universe Hypothesis

## Status
Speculative hypothesis permitted by UBCF; not established as a core axiom.

## Concept
A universe may potentially exist within a larger cosmological structure. One explored possibility is that black holes or other cosmological transitions in a parent universe could be associated with the formation of child universes.

Conceptually:

Parent Universe / Branch
→ Cosmological Transition
→ Nested Child Universe
→ Child Universe's Own Branching Structure

## Nested Universes and Ordinary Branches

A nested child universe is not treated as merely another ordinary branch inside the parent universe.

It is a causally descended structure produced by conditions in the parent causal structure. Once formed, the child universe may contain its own Event Branch Nodes, Causal Lineages, and branching tree.

The causal event associated with formation still belongs to the parent structure's history, but the resulting nested universe can be modeled as a new universal causal structure descended from that parent.

## Relative Physical Laws

Because a nested universe may originate under radically different cosmological conditions, UBCF permits the possibility that the physical laws, constants, geometries, or temporal descriptions experienced within it differ from those of the parent universe.

Causal ordering remains necessary for UBCF description even when local physical or temporal laws differ. A child universe may interpret or experience temporal direction differently while still possessing an ordered relation from causal predecessor to causal consequence.

This possibility is deliberately left open rather than asserted as established physics.

## Observational Limit
There is currently no UBCF claim that our universe has been demonstrated to exist inside a black hole. The idea is retained as a cosmological extension and research question.
