# Nature, Nurture, and Global Branching

## Purpose

Preserve the expansion of UBCF from individual decision branching into a universal causal model.

## Nature and Nurture

An individual does not develop from isolated personal choice alone. Genetics, upbringing, environment, social conditions, trauma, support, sensory experience, and the choices of other people all contribute to the individual's Causal Lineage.

Within UBCF, these influences are not background noise. They are causal information that helps determine the state from which later Event Branch Nodes occur.

## Global Branching

Branching is not personal and is not limited to events near an observer.

A causal event anywhere in reality can create branches, even if its effects will never reach Earth or any known observer.

Examples include:

- A distant stellar event.
- A collision between astronomical bodies.
- Biological development in another environment.
- A decision made by another individual on the opposite side of the world.

## Butterfly Effect

A remote event can later influence an observer through a long causal chain. Small changes may amplify into large differences over time.

This was one of the conceptual bridges from personal branching to universal branching.

## Sensory and Experiential Input

Every interaction experienced by an organism contributes information to its later state. Sensory experience therefore becomes part of the Causal Lineage that shapes later behavior and identity.

## Non-Anthropocentric Principle

Reality does not branch because humans make decisions. Human decision-making is one example of the broader rule that causality can have multiple distinguishable continuations.

UBCF therefore treats conscious action, biological processes, physical interactions, and cosmological events as different scales of the same branching principle.
