# Temporal Linearity and Paradox Rules

## Status
Historical development material that preserves rules established during the time-travel discussion.

## Time Does Not Loop
Within a branch, time is linear and moves forward.

A traveler may move backward to an earlier temporal coordinate, but this does not make time itself reverse or create a causal loop. The arrival produces a new branch whose own time then continues forward.

## No Self-Causing Loops
A traveler cannot become their own parent, grandparent, or earlier cause in a way that requires a closed causal loop.

If a traveler goes into the past and changes ancestry, the originating branch remains intact while the altered branch develops independently.

## Departure Symmetry
Leaving a branch creates a causal divergence:

- one continuation in which the traveler does not leave, and
- one continuation in which the traveler departs and is absent afterward.

The traveler's experienced continuation follows the departure history, but the unaltered continuation remains.

## Arrival Symmetry
Arrival also creates a causal divergence:

- one continuation in which the traveler does not arrive, and
- one continuation in which the traveler appears and becomes a new causal influence.

This applies to backward, forward, and sideways temporal displacement.

## Two Versions of the Traveler
Backward travel may produce a branch containing both a younger local version and an older arriving version.

They may share historical information up to earlier divergence, but after the travel event they are separate branch-local Identity Continuity Structures.

## Paradox Resolution Pattern
Classic paradoxes are resolved by the same general rule rather than by special exceptions:

1. existing causal history is not overwritten,
2. intervention produces a new branch,
3. branches remain independent, and
4. time within each resulting branch continues forward.
