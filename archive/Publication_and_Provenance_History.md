# Publication and Provenance History

## Status
Historical project record; not current UBCF canon.

## Purpose
This file preserves the earlier attempts to formalize, publish, timestamp, and protect the framework before the GitHub archive became the canonical record.

## Document Development
During the Copilot phase, multiple document forms were attempted, including:

- basic summaries,
- expanded philosophical models,
- research-style papers,
- formal theory drafts,
- diagram-supported versions,
- condensed book-style versions,
- short overviews,
- arXiv-style papers,
- Word documents, and
- LaTeX submission files.

Some early long-form drafts were flawed or repetitive and were later replaced by shorter, cleaner versions.

## arXiv Preparation
The project explored arXiv as a possible publication venue, especially categories associated with gravitation, cosmology, and general physics.

An arXiv-style paper was prepared with formal definitions, light mathematical notation, references, and LaTeX formatting.

Endorsement requirements became a practical obstacle, leading to discussion of contacting researchers working in related areas.

## Endorsement Research
Related areas explored included:

- Many-Worlds interpretations,
- branching cosmology,
- black-hole baby-universe hypotheses,
- time and causality in quantum gravity, and
- cosmological natural selection.

Names considered during that historical research included Lee Smolin, Edward J. Shaya, Dimiter Hadjimichef, César A. Zen Vasconcellos, Allic Sivaramakrishnan, and Allen Everett.

These names are preserved as historical leads, not as endorsements or claims that the researchers support UBCF.

## Protecting the Work
The project discussed ways to preserve provenance and public priority, including:

- timestamped drafts,
- emailing copies to oneself,
- version history,
- arXiv submission,
- OSF or Figshare uploads, and
- later, Git version control.

The GitHub repository ultimately became the preferred living archive because it preserves both content and change history.

## Transcript and Export Attempts
Several attempts were made to export the earlier conversation as project summaries, transcript-style records, and raw-text archives.

A complete verbatim reconstruction of unavailable historical chat messages was not possible. This limitation is one reason the current repository prioritizes structured preservation of every concept as it is recovered.
