# Time-Travel Paradox Examples

## Grandfather Paradox

A traveler who goes backward and changes events related to their ancestry does not erase their originating history.

The originating branch remains intact. The traveler's arrival creates a new branch in which later ancestry may develop differently and the younger branch-version of the traveler may never be born.

No causal loop is required.

## Encountering a Younger Self

If an older traveler encounters their younger self, both can coexist physically in the new branch.

They share an earlier Causal Lineage but are now separate Identity Continuity Structures with different experiences.

Physical contact between them has no special metaphysical effect; it simply becomes another causal interaction in the branch.

## The Two-Traveler Situation

After backward travel, a branch may contain both:

- the younger version progressing normally toward the travel event, and
- the older version who arrived from another causal history.

They are not one person occupying two temporal coordinates in a single unbroken identity path. They are distinct branch-local continuations sharing historical information up to earlier divergence.

## Returning Home

A traveler cannot restore the originating branch by recreating its events.

Even if a causality map allowed the traveler to reproduce the known sequence and appear at the exact instant of departure, the arrival itself adds a causal event and therefore creates another branch.

## Causal Alteration Preservation

In UBCF, an artificial intervention cannot overwrite an existing causal continuation. The unaltered continuation remains, while the intervention creates a new altered branch.

This means that traveling into a causal line in order to change it necessarily produces at least two continuations:

- one in which the traveler does not arrive and the prior causal history continues unaltered, and
- one in which the traveler arrives and the causal history is altered by that new event.

The intervention may radically change the altered branch, but it does not erase the continuation in which the intervention never occurred.

## "The One"-Style Elimination

Killing alternate versions of oneself cannot produce a single global survivor in UBCF.

The most fundamental reason is that the act of entering another causal line to kill a version of oneself is itself a causal alteration. That arrival creates a new branch. The continuation in which the traveler never arrived remains unaltered, while a separate branch is created in which the traveler arrives and may kill the local version.

Therefore, an attempt to eliminate an alternate self cannot reduce all branching reality to fewer versions. The intervention preserves the unaltered continuation and creates an altered continuation. Repeating the process creates further divergences rather than collapsing them.

Branches also do not merge, and energy or identity does not automatically transfer between them. A person may become the only surviving version known within one branch, but cannot become "the one" across all branching reality through branch-to-branch elimination.
