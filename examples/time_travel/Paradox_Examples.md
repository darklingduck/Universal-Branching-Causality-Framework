# Time-Travel Paradox Examples

## Grandfather Paradox

A traveler who goes backward and changes events related to their ancestry does not erase their originating history.

The originating branch remains intact. The traveler's arrival creates a new branch in which later ancestry may develop differently and the younger branch-version of the traveler may never be born.

No causal loop is required.

## Encountering a Younger Self

If an older traveler encounters their younger self, both can coexist physically in the new branch.

They share an earlier Causal Lineage but are now separate Identity Continuity Structures with different experiences.

Physical contact between them has no special metaphysical effect; it simply becomes another causal interaction in the branch.

## The Two-Traveler Situation

After backward travel, a branch may contain both:

- the younger version progressing normally toward the travel event, and
- the older version who arrived from another causal history.

They are not one person occupying two temporal coordinates in a single unbroken identity path. They are distinct branch-local continuations sharing historical information up to earlier divergence.

## Returning Home

A traveler cannot restore the originating branch by recreating its events.

Even if a causality map allowed the traveler to reproduce the known sequence and appear at the exact instant of departure, the arrival itself adds a causal event and therefore creates another branch.

## "The One"-Style Elimination

Killing alternate versions of oneself cannot produce a single global survivor in UBCF.

Branches do not merge, and energy or identity does not automatically transfer between them. A person may become the only surviving version known within one branch, but not "the one" across all branching reality.
